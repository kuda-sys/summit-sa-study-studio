Corrections made
----------------
- Replaced unnatural synonym-pair questions with direct, simple prompts about
  more formal alternatives, everyday words, and how vocabulary is used in a
  sentence.
- Rephrased textbook vocabulary prompts such as “Which example belongs to the
  word cookie?” into natural sentence/context questions.
- Simplified sentence proofreading prompts. The sentence to review is clearly
  introduced, followed by a direct question such as “Which sentence is correct?”
- Replaced the “clean-water tap beside the community centre” context with a
  more natural school setting.
- Changed “The visiting group” to “The visiting students” in plural-subject
  practice, avoiding the collective-noun ambiguity that caused the was/were
  answer-key complaint.
- Removed repeated past-time clues from map-based short-answer questions.
- Confirmed the answer key is one of the available choices for all 5,080 items.

Sound behavior implemented
--------------------------
- Each new quiz, reading-comprehension set, or listening-question set randomly
  selects one of the six supplied relaxing tracks and loops it at reduced volume.
- The relaxing track stops when the set is completed or the student exits that
  activity. A new set chooses a different track from the immediately previous
  one when possible.
- The supplied click sound plays on interactive controls such as buttons, tabs,
  links, and selectors.
  the next question. It can overlap the relaxing track.
- When a set's score is shown, the Correct answer sound plays only for a score
  strictly above 70%; otherwise the Wrong answer sound plays.

Important limitation
-------------------
These are automated structural and targeted common-sense checks across the
whole generated pool, plus targeted fixes for the examples reported. They do
not mean a teacher has manually proofread every randomized question one by one.
Please continue to report any question whose wording or factual context seems
unnatural so it can be corrected at source.

Limits
------
These checks cover syntax and focused logic, not every browser interaction.
Microphone access and the available speech-synthesis voice depend on the
student's browser, operating system, permissions, and security context. No claim
is made that a specific voice exists on every device or that every interaction
has been tested end-to-end in a live browser.
